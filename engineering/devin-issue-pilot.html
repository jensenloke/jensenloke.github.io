The Devin Issue Pilot

Every new issue in the parent repo starts a Devin session. Devin asks its questions on the issue, a reply from one of two named humans wakes it to carry on, and it opens PRs but never merges. The full flow, the guards, and the automation settings.

Jensen Loke · 5 October 2026 · ~6 min read

The idea

We already run Devin as a coding agent; the question this pilot answers is whether it can own the issue as well as the fix. The design: our sales lead files issues exactly as she does today - a screen name, a document number, a screenshot, no tags to remember. Every new issue in the parent repo triggers a Devin session. Devin reads the issue, the playbook and past fixes, then either asks numbered questions in plain English, flags that a business decision is needed, or claims the issue and works it. It opens PRs to dev/develop but never merges; I merge. After the merge, Devin verifies the dev deploy and posts test steps, and the issue only closes once the fix is confirmed working and recorded in the company brain.

Disclosure. I am a Devin ambassador. This describes a real production design; repository names, usernames, customer data and internal identifiers are omitted.

The flow

One issue, start to close.

  1. The sales lead opens an issue. Screen name, document number, screenshot. Nothing to tag.
  2. A Devin session starts. It reads the issue, the playbook, and past fixes.
  3. Triage. Labelled devin:skip, or claimed by another agent? Devin stays silent.
  4. Three exits from triage. Needs info → Devin comments numbered questions in plain English, with options to pick from. Needs a business decision → Devin comments Needs a decision, with the reason. Clear enough → Devin claims it: label agent:devin, status In progress.
  5. A reply wakes it. The sales lead or I answer in a comment, and Devin carries on: fixes, tests, self-reviews, opens a PR.
  6. PR ready. Devin comments PR ready for review, with links.
  7. I review and merge, then comment merged on the issue.
  8. Devin verifies dev. The comment wakes it to confirm the dev deploy and check it on dev.
  9. Ready to test. Devin comments test steps for the sales lead and sets the status Ready to test in dev.
  10. She tests. Works → she comments with a works screenshot; Devin records the fix in the company brain and closes the issue. Still broken → she comments what went wrong, and a new Devin session reads the thread and the handover notes.

What Devin posts on the issue

Four comment shapes, each with one job.

CommentAudienceContents
Question Sales lead Numbered, one decision each, options to pick from. No code terms. Reply with e.g. “1a”.
PR ready for review Me PR links, what changed, the tests Devin broke on purpose and what it could not verify.
Ready to test in dev Sales lead Where to click, which test document to use, what she should see.

A real question shape: one decision, lettered options, no code terms.

Guards

  • Only comments by the two of us wake Devin. Devin's own comments - and our support bot's - don't, so two bots can't loop.
  • One Devin at a time per issue. A working session holds the devin:working label and re-reads the thread before it stops, so a second quick reply is still answered.
  • Each session is capped at 30 ACUs. A comment on an issue Devin isn't handling costs only a short silent session. Devin writes its own comments, so a session with nothing to say posts nothing.
  • Same rules as today. PRs to dev/develop only, no merges, dev database only, never UAT or production.
  • Off switch for everything: disable the automation in Devin.

Decisions baked into the design

The points worth arguing about, and where they landed.

  1. Only the two of us can wake Devin with a comment. Everyone else's comments are read, not triggers.
  2. Issues filed by our support bot go to Devin too, unless the bot has claimed them with its own agent: label.
  3. After I merge, I comment merged on the issue. That wakes Devin to check dev and post the test steps.
  4. Devin closes the issue after the sales lead replies works - recording the fix in the company brain first.
  5. Smoke test: one issue titled [Devin pilot test], then close it. The sales lead may get a GitHub notification for it. Not yet checked: whether Devin's GitHub app can add labels in the parent repo - to be confirmed during the smoke test.
